The Medicines Your Members Will Ask You About

A plain rundown of the GLP-1s and peptides members bring up, so you can answer at the front desk without pretending to be a clinician. What each one is for, in one honest line.

You do not need to be a clinician to offer a referral program, but you do need to recognize the names members will say to you, because they will. Some of these are FDA-approved drugs and many of the peptides are not FDA-approved and are dispensed under the authority of a 503A compounding pharmacy, and you should be comfortable saying that plainly when asked. Your job is not to recommend or judge whether any of them is right for a person; that is the independent provider's call after a telemedicine evaluation. Your job is to know enough to point an interested member toward the right front door and let the clinician take it from there. Below is the plain-language map so you are never caught flat-footed when a member drops one of these names on you.

How Gym Owners Actually Add Peptides & GLP-1s

Owners ask, in plain terms, how does this actually work if I am not the one prescribing. Here is the whole thing in three steps. Step one, a member raises their hand: they ask about it at the front desk, tap something in your app, or sign up through your branded program. Step two, an independent licensed provider takes over: that MD or NP runs a telemedicine evaluation, looks at the member's history, and prescribes only if it is medically appropriate. Step three, a 503A compounding pharmacy ships the medication straight to the member's door. The member then trains in your gym and follows their plan. That is it. You never touch the medication, never store it, never ship it, and never make a medical decision about any individual. Your gym refers through its branded program and earns a referral margin, and the work you actually do is the part you already do every day, which is talk to members and coach them. The reason owners find this hard to believe at first is that it sounds like it should be more complicated, but the complexity lives with the providers and the pharmacy, not with you. Turnkey partners bundle the providers, the pharmacy, the software, and the staff training together, so you are connecting to a finished system rather than assembling one.

The Compliance & Referral Model

The question underneath all the others is, am I going to get in trouble, and the plain answer is that the referral model is built specifically to keep you out of trouble. In the United States, only licensed clinicians can practice medicine, and rules about the corporate practice of medicine are meant to keep non-clinicians like gym owners out of the exam room. Instead of fighting that, the model leans into it. You stay strictly in the referral lane, the independent licensed providers make every clinical decision and carry the clinical liability that goes with prescribing, and you are paid for the referral and for the branded program experience you deliver. It is structured so you are not splitting a clinical fee, which keeps you clear of fee-splitting concerns, and the provider and the pharmacy each stay independent. In practical owner terms: you are not diagnosing anyone, you are not deciding who gets a prescription, and you are not the one whose name is on the medical decision. That is the whole point. We are giving you the plain-English version here, not a legal treatise, and the specifics of any arrangement are worth running past your own attorney before you sign, because that is just good business. But the reason owners can offer this without losing sleep is that the lane you operate in, the member relationship and the training floor, is the one you already own, and the regulated lane stays with the people who are licensed for it.

Sourcing: 503A Pharmacies & Licensed Providers

A fair owner question is, where does the actual medicine come from, and am I responsible for any of it. You are not. On the clinical side, the program connects you to a network of independent licensed MDs and NPs who handle the evaluation and the prescription by telemedicine. On the fulfillment side, it connects you to a 503A compounding pharmacy that ships directly to the member. You do not buy anything, you do not stock anything, you do not arrange cold storage, you do not track expiration dates, and you do not run a shipping operation. That is exactly why a gym can go live quickly: there is no supply chain for you to build because you are plugging into one that already runs. Worth being honest about with members: many of the peptides are not FDA-approved and are dispensed under the compounding pharmacy's 503A authority, which is a legitimate path but is not the same as a mass-market FDA-approved drug, and your front desk should be able to say that plainly rather than dodge it. The medicine, where it comes from, how it is made, and how it gets to the member, is the pharmacy's and the provider's responsibility, not yours. Your only job on this front is to point interested members at the front door and let the licensed parties handle everything downstream of that.

What the Evidence Actually Supports for Members

Owners want to know, before they put their name on it, does this stuff actually work, and the honest answer has two halves. The GLP-1 medications, semaglutide and tirzepatide, have strong clinical evidence for weight loss; this is not a fad, people lose meaningful weight on them. The catch, and the reason a gym is genuinely the right place to offer them, is that rapid weight loss on these drugs can take lean muscle along with fat, not just fat alone. That is a real downside, and you should not hide it from members. It is also your edge: resistance training and enough protein are the proven counterweight to muscle loss, and that is precisely what your gym provides that a standalone telehealth app cannot. A member who loses weight under your roof keeps more strength and reshapes their body instead of just shrinking. Be equally honest on the other half: the recovery and longevity peptides have limited human evidence, and many of them are not FDA-approved and are dispensed under 503A compounding authority. None of this is a promise of any particular result for any particular member. The reason being honest here helps you rather than hurts you is that members can smell hype, and an owner who tells the truth about both the upside and the limits is the owner they trust with something as personal as their body.

Peptides & GLP-1s That Come Up Most in Gyms

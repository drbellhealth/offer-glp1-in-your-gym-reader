Offering GLP-1 in Your Gym: An Owner Q and A

Straight, owner-to-owner answers for the gym owner who is on the fence about offering GLP-1 and peptide therapy. Do you need a medical license, how long does it take to launch, how much can you realistically make, and what happens if a member has a problem. Plain answers, no hype.

If you own a gym and you have been watching members ask about semaglutide and tirzepatide, you have probably wondered whether you should be the one offering it. You have also probably hesitated, because the whole thing sounds like it might require a clinic, a nurse, a license you do not have, and a level of legal risk you did not sign up for. That hesitation is fair, and it is exactly the reason this page is written as a plain question-and-answer instead of a sales pitch. We are going to answer the real questions an owner asks before deciding, not the questions a vendor wishes you would ask. The short version is that the model most gyms use is a referral, which means you do not prescribe, you do not stock medication, you do not ship anything, and you do not carry the clinical risk. Independent licensed providers handle the medical side by telemedicine, a compounding pharmacy ships to the member, and your gym refers members through its own branded program and earns a referral margin. That changes almost every answer below, so it is worth understanding before you read further.

The Questions Owners Ask Before They Say Yes

When an owner sits down to seriously think about this, the same handful of questions come up every time, and they are the right ones. The first is almost always about the license: do I, as a non-clinician, need any medical credential to offer this. The honest answer is no, not in the referral model, because you never practice medicine, and that one fact removes the biggest perceived barrier right away. The second question is time: how long until I can actually launch. Because you are plugging into an existing provider network and pharmacy rather than building a clinic, the partner side can be live quickly, often within a day or two of the setup call, and your first member can come through the same week. The third question is money, and here the honest answer is that you can earn a referral margin on members who enroll, but any specific figure is illustrative and not typical, and there are no guarantees, so anyone who hands you a guaranteed number is not being straight with you. The fourth question is demand: will my members actually want this. For most gyms the answer is that some already do, because they are asking about GLP-1s whether you offer them or not, and the only real question is whether they get it from you or from a random telehealth ad. The fifth is about staffing and inventory: do I need to hire a nurse or stock medication. No on both counts, because the clinical work and the fulfillment both sit with your partners, not with you. The sixth is legal exposure: am I taking on liability. The clinical liability sits with the licensed providers who prescribe, and the structure is built so you are paid for the referral and the branded experience, not for splitting a clinical fee. The seventh is brand: does offering this cheapen my gym or strengthen it, and that one is genuinely yours to decide based on your members and your positioning. Work through those seven honestly and you will know whether this is a yes for you.

So, Should You Offer It?

Here is the honest owner-to-owner take. If your members are already asking about GLP-1s, if you are comfortable being the person who routes them to a legitimate licensed provider instead of letting them buy something sketchy online, and if you like the idea of adding a revenue line that does not require you to become a clinic, then offering it through a referral program is worth a serious look. You do not need a license, you do not hire a nurse, you do not stock or ship medication, and the clinical risk stays with the providers who prescribe. The setup is fast because you are plugging into something that already exists rather than building it. Where you should slow down is on expectations: do not let anyone sell you a guaranteed income number, because revenue is illustrative and not typical and depends entirely on your members and your follow-through. Slow down too if your brand is built on a message that would clash with offering a weight loss medication, because that is a real tension only you can judge. And go in clear-eyed about the medicine: GLP-1s genuinely work for weight loss, the lean-muscle-loss risk is real and is exactly why a gym is a good home for the offer, and many peptides are not FDA-approved and are dispensed under 503A compounding authority. If you can say yes to the members, yes to the model, and yes to being honest about all of it, then this fits. If any of those is a no, it is fine to pass. None of this is medical, legal, or financial advice, and the decision is yours.

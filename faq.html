The Questions Owners Actually Ask

Blunt answers to the decision questions owners ask first, from licensing and timing to money, staffing, liability, and what happens if a member has a problem.

Do I need a medical license to offer this?

No. This is a referral model, and you never practice medicine. Independent licensed MDs and NPs handle every telemedicine evaluation and prescription, and a 503A compounding pharmacy handles fulfillment. You make a branded referral and provide the training environment, nothing more. Because you do not prescribe, diagnose, store medication, or carry clinical liability, no medical license is required of you as the gym owner to offer or run the program.

How long does it take to launch?

Fast, because you are plugging into an existing provider network and pharmacy rather than building one. The partner side can be live quickly, often within a day or two of the setup call, since there is nothing for you to procure or warehouse. Your first member can come through the same week. The thing that actually paces your launch is your own side, naming the program, setting up the simple intake, and briefing your staff, not the medical infrastructure, which is already built.

How much money can a gym realistically make?

You earn a referral margin on members who enroll, and across a membership that adds up, but here is the honest part: any specific number is illustrative and not typical, and there are no guarantees. It depends entirely on your members, your pricing, and your follow-through. If anyone hands you a guaranteed income figure, be skeptical, because they cannot know that. Think of it as a real new revenue line, not a windfall, and size it against your own membership rather than a vendor's example.

Will my members actually want this?

Many already do, whether you offer it or not. Members are searching for GLP-1s online right now, so for most gyms the real question is not whether the demand exists but whether your members get it from you, with a real gym and real coaching behind it, or from an anonymous ad. The best way to judge is to picture your own floor: if people are already asking about semaglutide or tirzepatide at the front desk, that is your answer.

Do I have to hire anyone?

Generally no. Your existing front-desk and training team can surface the branded program and make the referral as part of normal member conversations, which is why briefing them and giving them a short script is a quick task rather than a hiring project. The clinical work is handled by the independent providers you plug into, so you are not hiring a nurse, a coordinator, or a prescriber. Turnkey partners also bundle the staff training, so your team is not figuring it out alone.

Do I have to carry any inventory?

No. You stock no medication, store nothing, and build no pharmacy or clinic. The 503A compounding pharmacy ships directly to the member, so there is no cold storage, no expiration dates to track, no purchasing, and no shipping operation on your side. That is a big part of why this is so much lighter than adding equipment or square footage: your startup effort is configuration and communication, not capital tied up in product sitting on a shelf.

What is my legal exposure?

The clinical liability sits with the licensed providers who evaluate and prescribe, not with you, because you never make a medical decision. The model is structured so you are paid for the referral and the branded experience rather than splitting a clinical fee, which keeps you clear of fee-splitting concerns, and the provider and pharmacy each stay independent. This is the plain-English version, not legal advice; run the specific arrangement past your own attorney before signing, which is simply good business for any new offer.

What happens if a member has a problem?

It goes to the licensed provider who prescribed and is responsible for that member's care, not to your front desk, because you were never the clinician. That is the whole reason the referral structure protects both the member and you. The provider may also decide before any prescription that a member is not a candidate or should address something else first, which is the system working as intended. Train your staff to refer and step back whenever there is any doubt; protecting the member protects your brand.
